# Prior art and testable innovation

Reviewed 2026-11-07. **TipCraft's strongest current contribution is a reproducible test of whether published tail-fiber and host-recognition evidence supports useful, transferable predictions.** A successful project can establish a bounded capability or identify its failure conditions. The biological platform hypothesis is a reusable recognition interface within a defined scaffold family; its scope must emerge from evidence.

This review informs the modular-platform ambition and the three project levels recorded in [the project plan](00-project-plan.md). Current work remains retrospective. TipCraft is the software name; the team has not selected the biological project name.

## Closest biological prior art

| Primary source and publication date | Established contribution | Implication for TipCraft |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Garcia-Doval & van Raaij, **2012-05-29** online, [T7 gp17 C-terminal structure](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1119719109), Conclusion; [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3386108/) | A crystallized gp17 fragment contains structural domains and suggests receptor-binding regions. | Structural modularity provides a starting description. A fragment structure supplies neither an interchangeable interface specification nor a success rate for replacement. |
| Ando et al., **2015-09-23**, [Engineering Modular Viral Scaffolds for Targeted Bacterial Population Editing](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cels.2015.08.013), “Swapping tail fibers” and Fig. 3; [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4785837/) | Published full-fiber and C-terminal exchanges alter host-range phenotypes in related phages. The study also reports differing infectivity between full-fiber and C-terminal versions carrying material from the same donor. | Modular scaffold engineering and T7/T3 exchange are established prior art. Donor identity alone is insufficient to specify the resulting phenotype. The current ten observations capture only part of this publication. |
| Yehl et al., **2019-10-03** online, [Engineering Phage Host-Range and Suppressing Bacterial Resistance through Phage Tail Fiber Mutagenesis](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2019.09.015), Summary and Results; [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6924272/) | Structure-informed diversification of T3 host-range-determining regions produced phagebodies with altered host ranges and experimentally assessed resistance suppression. | A common recognition scaffold carrying functional diversity is established. The result concerns selected variants in a particular system; it does not demonstrate interchangeability of arbitrary external binders. |
| Dunne et al., **2019-10-29**, [Reprogramming Bacteriophage Host Range through Structure-Guided Design of Chimeric Receptor Binding Proteins](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2019.09.062), Summary; [primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31665644/) | Structure-guided exchanges of RBP domains in the Listeria PSA system produced chimeric phages with changed host ranges. | Domain-based recognition engineering extends beyond T7/T3. Transferability depends on the demonstrated structural and biological context; Listeria results do not calibrate gp17 compatibility. |
| Ogari & Yehl, **2025-12-01** online; **2026-01-16** issue, [Engineering T3 and T7 host range to target protein receptors and guide bacterial evolution](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2025.114299), Figs. 2–4 and Discussion; [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12804162/) | A defined antigen–nanobody recognition system enabled protein-receptor targeting in engineered T3/T7 contexts. The phage displayed an antigen and the bacterial surface displayed its complementary nanobody. T7 tail-fiber display failed to extend host range in the tested setting, whereas a T7/T3 hybrid succeeded. Their AlphaFold comparisons showed no apparent difference explaining this contrast. | This is direct prior art for adding protein-mediated recognition to these LPS-targeting phages. The scaffold-dependent negative result is valuable retrospective evidence. The study does not test arbitrary de novo binders grafted onto gp17 or establish an assembly classifier. |

Native-receptor interpretation comes from [González-García et al., 2015-02-19 online](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.M114.614222), Abstract and Results: T7 fibers recognize rough LPS, which triggers DNA ejection in vitro. A scoring method for native T7 must explain how its inputs relate to this glycan context. Biosynthesis proteins can provide statistical features while representing a different physical entity from the surface receptor. The artificial protein-recognition system above addresses a separate, engineered context.

## Software prior art and the useful gap

| Existing capability | Primary source | Contribution TipCraft would need to add |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Automated tail-fiber segmentation, multimeric structure prediction and structural classification | Klein-Sousa et al., **2025-06-06**, [RBPseg: Toward a complete phage tail fiber structure atlas](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adv0870); [full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12143355/), Results and Discussion | A validated relationship between structural features and a stated compatibility endpoint. RBPseg reports structural classes, modularity and prediction limitations; structure quality alone does not supply a functional assembly label. |
| Automated de novo protein-binder design | Pacesa et al., **2025-08-27**, [One-shot design of functional protein binders with BindCraft](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09429-6), Abstract and Fig. 1 | Evidence that a separately evaluated binder retains the relevant behavior in a tail-fiber context. The published binder endpoint does not estimate that transfer probability. Binder generation remains a future capability for TipCraft. |
| Modular orchestration of existing protein-design and assessment tools | Silke et al., **2026-01-21**, [ProteinDJ: A high-performance and modular protein design pipeline](https://doi.org/10.1002/pro.70464), Abstract | A phage-specific evaluation contract and demonstrated practical benefit. Connecting existing tools or adding a graphical interface, by itself, is a software integration contribution. |
| Genome-based strain-level phage–host prediction, including unseen-phage assessment | Noonan et al., **2026-09-29**, [Phylogeny-agnostic strain-level prediction of phage–host interactions from genomes using machine learning](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41564-026-02482-5), Results and Methods; [GenoPHI implementation](https://github.com/Noonanav/GenoPHI) | An independently audited evaluation on the exact retrospective task, with coverage, relatedness exclusions and appropriate baselines. GenoPHI supplies a conditional candidate for that work; [input readiness remains HOLD](09-next-method-feasibility.md). It does not establish gp17 assembly prediction. |

These sources establish that modular recognition engineering, tail-fiber structural workflows, binder design and strain-level host prediction already exist. TipCraft can combine their outputs into a useful research instrument by preserving source identity and evaluating each claimed capability against its own evidence. A first-ever platform claim would require a broader literature, software, patent and prior-iGEM search than this bounded review.

## Innovation hypotheses and evidence that would change the direction

| Proposed contribution | Evidence needed to earn the claim | Falsifier or decision consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Independent scoring adds information about strain specificity.** | Freeze an executable method and external training/exclusion specification before new scores. Evaluate exact supported inputs against tied/random and historical WIsH rankings on identical covered cases. Follow [the next-method contract](../software/tipcraft/benchmarks/retro-t7-t3/next-method-contract.json). | No improvement on the informative comparisons, unresolved training overlap, or missing authenticated inputs keeps the ranking capability at HOLD/NO-GO. The existing WIsH negative result remains part of the evidence. A win on the tiny native panel is a diagnostic signal requiring independent follow-up. |
| **A useful compatibility rule can be learned from published modular examples.** | Curate source-mapped compatible and incompatible cases with authenticated molecular inputs and separately identified binding, oligomerization, incorporation and infection observations. Test a rule on grouped held-out publications and related constructs. | If published data contain only selected successes, or only infection outcomes, an assembly-specific predictor cannot be assessed. A structure metric that cannot distinguish published contrasting outcomes does not support compatibility ranking. |
| **A reusable interface has a defensible operating domain.** | Define the scaffold family, recognition-module classes, endpoint and allowed contextual assumptions in advance. Assess published transfers across that domain, including failures and missing observations. | Recurrent dependence on donor/scaffold context narrows the domain. Evidence for several successful swaps supports those transfers; a universal interface remains an untested extrapolation beyond them. |
| **TipCraft improves researchers' decisions.** | A reproducible report links every input and score to provenance, displays abstentions and failure cases, and helps users identify which conclusions the data support. Compare practical reproducibility and interpretation with using upstream outputs directly. | If users cannot reproduce the report or distinguish prediction endpoints, the integration has not delivered its intended value. Missing data and a justified HOLD can still be useful outputs when their reasons are traceable. |

## Roadmap consequence

Prioritize the independent host-scoring evaluation and a catalogue of published compatibility contrasts. Keep the six native observations, four construct abstentions and WIsH failure visible. Add further cases only after source and endpoint review; lock their role and grouping before producing new results. The recent T3/T7 display contrast is a candidate for curation, with its own input-identity audit, rather than an automatic addition to the present score matrix.

A later biological platform claim should name the tested operating domain and the measured endpoint. Standalone binding, assembled-fiber compatibility and functional infection each need their own evidence. The December decision should select the strongest supported contribution and the next uncertainty to resolve, using these hypotheses and their falsifiers.
